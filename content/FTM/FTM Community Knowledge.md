Trans people have been figuring out testosterone together for decades, long before any guidelines caught up, and often more carefully than the clinicians writing them. This page is a map of that knowledge.

## The community was right first

Again and again, transmasc community knowledge *preceded* formal medicine by years.

| Community practice/report | What the community said                                                                                | Where medical papers caught up                                                                                                |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **SubQ injections**       | Belly/thigh fat injections with small needles work and hurt less (standard since the 2000s, off-label) | Olson 2014; Spratt 2017; UCSF endorsement; FDA-approved SubQ device (Xyosted, 2018)                                           |
| **Weekly dosing**         | "Shot-day crash" at the end of 2-4 week cycles; weekly shots fix it                                    | Endocrine Society TRT guideline (weekly preferred); WADA 2023 guidance                                                        |
| **Trough testing**        | Draw blood right *before* your shot to catch low points                                                | Formalized in Endocrine Society/UCSF/WADA timing guidance                                                                     |
| **Pelvic pain on T**      | Forum reports for years pre-2015; dismissed as rare                                                    | Now documented at 23% (adolescents), 36% (clinic sample), up to 69-72% in community surveys; dedicated UCSF guideline section |
| **"T voice"**             | Persistent cracking/instability in year 1; some voices never fully settle                              | Nygren 2016 (F0 drops regardless of levels); >60% instability on long-interval undecanoate; "entrapped voice" concept         |

## Validated practices

**Subcutaneous/SubQ (into the fat under the skin) injection works and people prefer it.** In the key study (Spratt 2017, n=63 trans men), weekly SubQ cypionate/enanthate (the two common injectable forms of T) put **all** participants in the male range, 51/53 achieved amenorrhea (periods stopping), and **22 of 22** who'd tried both routes preferred SubQ.[^1] UCSF now lists SubQ benefits (smaller, less painful needle; avoids decades of IM scarring).[^2]

**Weekly beats biweekly.** Injectable esters every 2-4 weeks produce supraphysiologic peaks and sub-physiologic troughs (levels spiking above the natural range, then crashing below it); guidance for cis and trans patients alike says to shorten the interval when people report end-of-cycle fatigue, low mood, irritability, or return of bleeding.[^3]

**Amenorrhea is dose- and interval-dependent.** Menses (periods) stop faster at adequate levels: in adolescent SubQ data, cessation was 54% at 140 mg/month vs 97% at 200 mg/month; in Deutsch 2015, 16% still menstruated at 6 months.[^4][^5] The community heuristic is "if your period comes back before shot day, your interval is too long or your dose too low" which matches studied evidence.

## Underdosing

Community forums are full of stories of very low starts, slow titration (gradual dose increases), and providers refusing increases:

- In a prospective (following people forward in time) study of 31 trans men on *guideline-typical* starting regimens, **only 68% had male-range testosterone at 6 months** so roughly a third were sub-therapeutic (and only 29% had male-range estradiol, the main estrogen).[^5]
- UCSF itself warns that skipping level checks "runs the risk of a suboptimal degree of virilization."[^2]
- Symptomatic underdosing: breakthrough bleeding and pelvic pain are associated with *lower* T levels.[^6]
- **Monitoring tempo:** New Zealand's PATHA protocol deliberately doesn't check the first T level until 9-12 months ("it takes time for levels to stabilise") while UCSF checks at 3-6 months and community practice is a trough draw (blood taken just before shot day) at ~3 months. Under the slow model, a non-responder can spend a year sub-therapeutic.[^7]

If your levels are low and you're not seeing changes, you're allowed to ask for a level check and a dose review that's not being a "difficult patient", it's vital for your health. More at [[FTM Dosages#Underdosing]] and [[FTM Blood Testing]].

## Info with less professional data

**Genital T/DHT cream ("bottom growth cream").** 10% DHT (dihydrotestosterone, which is a stronger hormone made from testosterone) cream applied for ~3 months *before metoidioplasty* (a type of bottom surgery) is an established surgical protocol (Belgrade school; pediatric DHT data exist too).[^8] for non-surgical cosmetic growth: no trial shows compounded cream adds growth beyond what systemic T does, genital skin absorbs systemically.[^8]

**Minoxidil for beard growth.** Exactly one trans-specific case report (a 17-year-old who grew pigmented facial hair in 3 months on 5% minoxidil with body-hair growth beyond the application site, suggesting systemic absorption), one 16-person cohort showing significant gains, and one small cis-male RCT (randomized controlled trial).[^9][^10] The case report's authors explicitly credit online forums with using it first. Whether gains persist after stopping is unknown (cis data suggest regression within 3-4 months). keep minoxidil away from cats (it's lethal to felines) and expect a shedding phase.

## The harm-reduction testing gap

Black-market steroid analysis finds ~36% of products counterfeit and ~37% substandard.[^12] Community harm-reduction orgs lab-test grey-market hormones to catch this such as [Trans Harm Reduction publishes its results](https://transharmreduction.org/hrt-testing) but their published testing has been **almost entirely estradiol**. Testosterone's public testing footprint is essentially one vial ever (a 2023 testosterone cypionate sample measuring 233 mg/mL vs 250 advertised, with impurities).[^13] Transmasculine people who self-source rely on steroid UGLs (underground labs) that trans harm-reduction barely tests. If you self-source, blood monitoring through a GP matters even more; see [[FTM Access]] for harm-reduction pointers, and the [diyhrt.wiki transmasc guide](https://diyhrt.wiki/transmasc) for the community's practical safety rundown.

## What community knowledge can't do

Community wisdom is strongest at *describing lived experience* and weakest at *estimating risk*. It told us about pelvic pain, trough crashes, and T voice and was right. But only studies could show that a third of people whose periods have stopped still ovulate, that 11% develop erythrocytosis (too many red blood cells). Use both kinds of knowledge for what they're good at and be wary of any source (clinical *or* community) that claims certainty the evidence doesn't support. More on that in [[FTM Evidence Gaps]].

## Further reading

No single website - this one included - should be your only source; these are reputable places to keep reading.

- [Trans Harm Reduction - HRT source testing](https://transharmreduction.org/hrt-testing) - lab results for grey-market hormones (mostly estradiol so far - the gap this page describes).
- [diyhrt.wiki - Transmasc HRT](https://diyhrt.wiki/transmasc) - the community-maintained DIY reference for testosterone.
- [Hudson's FTM Resource Guide - Testosterone and health](https://www.ftmguide.org/tandhealth.html) - the classic community T handbook (older, but foundational).
- [UCSF Transgender Care - Overview of masculinizing hormone therapy](https://transcare.ucsf.edu/guidelines/masculinizing-therapy) - where a lot of community practice ended up clinically validated.
- [Spratt et al. 2017 - Subcutaneous vs intramuscular testosterone in trans men (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28379417/) - the study that proved the community right on SubQ.
